# Before and after: rewrites from our own content

Each pair keeps the facts and changes only the habits.

**Meta intro**
- Before: "This piece covers what the data says, which query types lose and keep clicks, how to adjust your content, and how to measure AI traffic without fooling yourself."
- After: cut it. The first paragraph already states the problem and the answer; headings show the rest.

**Significance word**
- Before: "That second number matters: it suggests people are also getting answers elsewhere."
- After: "So clicks fell even where Google showed no AI answer, which points to people asking chat assistants before they search."

**Throat-clearing summary line**
- Before: "Three independent sources point the same way."
- After: start with the first source. If the agreement is the point, say it after the evidence: "Pew, Ahrefs and Seer measured this differently and still landed in the same place."

**Negative parallelism in a heading**
- Before: "## Write pages that get cited, not just ranked"
- After: "## Getting quoted inside the AI answer"

**"Less X, more Y"**
- Before: "So the question is less 'will there be an AI answer' and more 'will the AI answer finish the job'."
- After: "Assume there will be an AI answer. What decides your clicks is whether that answer finishes the job."

**Bold-led paragraphs**
- Before: "**Pew Research Center** tracked the browsing of 900 US adults… / **Ahrefs** compared… / **Seer Interactive** tracked…"
- After: plain paragraphs that name the source in the sentence: "In March 2025, Pew Research Center tracked the browsing of 900 US adults."

**Templated worked example**
- Before (in 15 posts): "Say you spend ₹2,00,000 a month…"
- After: vary the framing and only use an example where it earns its place: "A Pune skincare brand spending ₹2 lakh a month on Meta…", "Take a SaaS blog with 200 posts.", or a table of the numbers with no lead-in.

**Templated ending**
- Before (in 21 posts): "## What to do this week" followed by five bullets.
- After: an ending that fits the piece: a decision rule ("If your CPA is above X, stop here"), a short checklist under a specific heading ("Before you switch on Advantage+"), or no closing section at all when the last section already lands the point.

**Rule of three**
- Before: "comparisons, decisions, tools, local needs, brand searches and problems specific enough…"
- After: keep long lists only when each item is needed; otherwise pick the two strongest and give an example.
